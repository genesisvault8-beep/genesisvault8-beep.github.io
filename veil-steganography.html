The Science of Secrets
01
Zero-Width Characters
Unicode contains invisible characters that take up no visual space. Two of them — Zero-Width Space (U+200B) and Zero-Width Non-Joiner (U+200C) — can represent binary 0 and 1.
0 → U+200B (Zero-Width Space)
1 → U+200C (Zero-Width Non-Joiner)
Both are completely invisible to readers
02
Binary Encoding
Every character in your secret message is converted to its 16-bit binary representation, then each bit becomes one invisible zero-width character.
"Hi" → 72, 105
72 → 0000000001001000
105 → 0000000001101001
= 32 invisible characters
03
Injection
The entire stream of invisible characters is inserted directly after the visible emoji. The result looks identical to the original — one innocent emoji.
😊 + [invisible bits]
= 😊 (visually identical)
but carries hidden data
works on all platforms
04
Optional Encryption
When a password is set, the message is first encrypted using XOR cipher with a key derived from your password, then encoded as zero-width characters.
message + password
→ XOR encrypt
→ binary stream
→ zero-width inject
Detection
Most platforms — Twitter, WhatsApp, Instagram, Discord, iMessage — pass zero-width characters through untouched. The emoji appears completely normal in every feed, chat, and post. No tool can detect the hidden data without knowing to look for zero-width characters specifically. Even then, without the password, the decrypted content is gibberish.

Note: some platforms (Telegram, Signal) may strip zero-width characters. Test before use in sensitive contexts.
